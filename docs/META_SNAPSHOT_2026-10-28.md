# M-C meta snapshot — 28 October 2026

**292 tournaments · 16,068 teams · events from 09-09 to 10-27.**
Tournament teamlists from Limitless; builds and spreads from Champions Battle Data
(ranked ladder, season M7).

The sample is now **7.5x** the 19 September snapshot (38 events, 2,148 teams), and it
covers the whole regulation rather than its first nine days. That changes what this
document can say: September's was a reading of an opening week, this one has four
clean weekly data points either side of a genuine turnover.

Four things to hold while reading:

- **The week of 10-26 is partial** — 11 events, only 26 and 27 October landed. Do not
  read its column as a trend.
- **That week also contains a themed event.** The Spooky Cup (230 players, 31% of the
  week's teams) is a Halloween event: Sinistcha 42.6%, Tyranitar 37.8%, Grimmsnarl,
  Corviknight, Scrafty. Strip it out and the rest of the week is continuous with the
  one before it. It is the reason every headline species appears to fall off a cliff
  in the last column, and it is an artifact, not a meta move.
- **Spreads come from the ladder, not from tournaments.** Limitless teamlists carry
  no EVs, so every spread below is the ranked ladder's most common *full* spread.
  Different population, same regulation — treat them as the common build, not as
  what the player across from you is definitely holding.
- **Build detail is keyed by species, not forme.** Garchomp's modal item reads
  `Garchompite Z` because the Mega-Z users are in the same row. Plain Garchomp does
  exist (it carries the Scarf flag), it is just not the modal build.

**Salamence-Mega has collapsed.** It was the third-most-brought Pokémon in September
at 37.5% and the anchor of the format. It is now under 9% and still falling, monotone
across all seven complete weeks. Nothing else in the data moves that far.

**The replacement is a speed-and-power core, not another bulky Mega.** Raichu-Mega-Y
and Garchomp both roughly tripled. They are the two fastest things in the top ten
(Raichu 200, Garchomp-Mega-Z 203 / Scarf Garchomp 231), and both arrived in the same
two-week stretch Salamence left.

**Rain is the quiet story.** Pelipper +5.5 and Archaludon +12.6, and the two pair at
lift 5.73. Charizard rising at the same time means both weathers are up while the
format's old top end is down.

Over the whole window, the **both-weathers team** flagged as an oddity in September —
a rain setter *and* a sun setter on the same six — has gone from **1.7% of teams to
3.9% (628 of 16,068)**, counted with September's exact definition (Pelipper alongside
Charizard or Venusaur) so the two numbers mean the same thing. It more than doubled
while the sample grew 7.5x. That was worth watching, and it is still worth watching.

### Things in that table worth acting on

**The format is overwhelmingly 2/32/32.** Eleven of sixteen run some variant of
max-offence / max-Speed with two points parked in HP. Only Incineroar, Farigiraf,
Volcarona and Archaludon are built to take hits, and all four are support.

**Rillaboom and Archaludon are the trap.** Both show a 150 max, both are *actually*
105. Those are the two biggest ceiling-to-likely gaps in the format and the two
places where investing Speed to "outrun the threat" buys you almost nothing — you
would be paying 8–10 SP to beat a build roughly a third of them run.

**Raichu-Mega-Y's likely equals its max — 200.** Everybody runs it max Speed, so
there is no hedging: you are either over 200 or you are not. It also has **No Guard
Zap Cannon**, a 100% paralysis that cannot miss.

**Kingambit runs Chople Berry**, which is a direct answer to the Fighting moves that
Sneasler (35%) threatens it with.

## 6. Team Mogbla against this meta, on paper

`Kingambit / Froslass / Primarina / Sneasler / Garchomp / Charizard`

### What is good

**No flagged types and no coverage holes.** Against the recent top 20, there is not a
single threat that nobody on the team hits super effectively, and there is no type
where three or more members are weak with nothing resisting. That holds under all
three Mega choices. The September snapshot's example team had holes; this one does
not, and that is a genuinely strong structural result.

**The Speed tier is high.** Scarf Garchomp at 231 is faster than everything in the
format except an opposing Jolly Scarf Garchomp (253). Mega Froslass at 189 and
Sneasler at 172 are both in the top bracket.

### What is harder

**Carrying two Mega stones is normal — the cost here is which two.**

First, the thing that is *not* a problem, stated plainly because an earlier draft of
this section got it wrong: **78.0% of teams in the sample carry two or more Mega
stones** (78.3% carry exactly two; only 21% carry one). You Mega once per game, so a
second stone is a Team Preview option, not a wasted slot. That is the format's
standard practice, not a flaw.

What *is* unusual is carrying two Megas that both **set weather**:

- **Froslass-Mega has Snow Warning** — megaing it sets snow, which is what turns on
  Aurora Veil.
- **Charizard-Mega-Y has Drought** — megaing it sets sun, which Heat Wave, Weather
  Ball and a one-turn Solar Beam are built around.

**Only 44 teams in 16,068 (0.3%) carry two weather-setting Megas, and 19 (0.12%)
carry this exact pair.** Rarity is not badness — it may just be unexplored — but it
does mean there is no body of results to learn from.

The concrete cost is that the stone you *don't* use takes more off its holder than it
usually would. On most two-stone teams the unmegad Pokémon is still a functioning
attacker. Here:

- **Froslass unmegad loses a move outright.** Aurora Veil cannot be used without snow,
  and nothing else on the team sets it. That is a dead slot, not a weaker one.
- **Charizard unmegad is weakened but still works.** Heat Wave is a fine spread Fire
  move off Blaze; Weather Ball drops to 50 BP Normal and Solar Beam wants its charge
  turn back.

So the asymmetry is the real finding: megaing Froslass costs Charizard power, while
megaing Charizard costs Froslass a whole move.

**Froslass is the liability either way:**

| | OHKO'd by |
|---|---|
| Froslass (base, when Charizard megas) | **13 of 20** |
| Froslass-Mega (when it megas) | 10 of 20 |

At 147 HP base it is the frailest thing on the team by a distance. Volcarona's
Overheat does 229–269% and Gholdengo's Make It Rain 203–241%.

**You win very few damage races outright.** Against the top 20, the team has a
guaranteed OHKO that lands through maximum defensive investment on only **2 of 20**
threats. Several key matchups sit in the uncomfortable 90–110% band where the roll
decides:

```
Archaludon   best is Sneasler Close Combat     92-110%
Gholdengo    best is Kingambit Kowtow Cleave   95-113%
Pelipper     best is Primarina Moonblast       89-106%
Volcarona    best is Garchomp Rock Slide       86-102%
Farigiraf    best is Kingambit Kowtow Cleave   66-78%
```

**Sneasler is the biggest single problem.** It scores 102.0 on the pressure ranking —
over half again the next threat — and hits 4 of your 6 super effectively. It is also
35% of the field and it moves at 172, exactly tying your own Sneasler.

### Worst incoming hits

```
Farigiraf    Psychic       -> Sneasler    232-275%
Volcarona    Overheat      -> Froslass    229-269%
Indeedee-F   Psychic       -> Sneasler    209-247%
Gholdengo    Make It Rain  -> Froslass    203-241%
Sylveon      Hyper Beam    -> Garchomp    188-223%
Sneasler     Close Combat  -> Kingambit   187-222%
```

Sneasler being erased by any Psychic move from two separate common threats is worth
planning around, given it is also your Fake Out lead.

## 7. What I would watch

1. **Whether Salamence's floor has been found.** It is monotone down over seven weeks
   but still on 20% of winners. If it stabilises, the teams built to beat it are
   mispriced.
2. **Rain.** Pelipper, Archaludon and the Basculegion-F core are all rising together,
   and Archaludon is the format's best over-performer that nobody is calling a threat.
3. **Raichu-Mega-Y.** 25% of winners, max Speed on every build, No Guard Zap Cannon.
   It is the most reliable winner in the data and the hardest to interact with.
4. **Both-weather teams at 4.0% and doubling.** September flagged this at 1.7% as a
   curiosity. It is no longer a curiosity.

And the thing this snapshot still cannot tell you: usage and placement are the shadow,
not the shape. None of it accounts for *how* these teams win.
